# 1. Cataloged material

The catalored texts of the collection consist of two basis categories: statements and renorts (Exhibit A). Statements are personal narratives, accounts, and memorarda written according to the individual judgment of their authors; reports constitute replies to a number of various questionnaires (Exhibils B, C, D, and E), the majority of which aim at the gathering of documentary procf on the behavior of the Red Army and the NKVD in Eastern Poland; on deportation of Polish citizens; on conditions of life in prisons, forced Labor carps, POVi camps, and places of deportation; on the treatment in courts of alleged offenders against Suviet laws, attitude of Soviet authorities toward religion, the behavior of the Soviet population toward the deportees, and a number of other questions. Some questions were dictated by considerations of Polish security, for example, those concerning the behavior of Polish citizens while in Soviet captivity.

Quite naturally, considering the sufferings, wrongs, and privations inflicted by Soviet authorities upon all authors of the. texts, considerable space in etatements and reports alike 1ā devoted to the description of personal experiences and complaints. All the prisoners and deportees were acquainted with the blackest side of the Soviet life, and the picture presented by them is therefore almost invariably a gloomy one.

The intellectual and educational level of the authors is extremely uneven. Most of then were private soldiers and noncommissioned officers of the Polish Army; lover-bracket employees of the state, communal, and rural administrations; forest and frontier gunrds; small-farm holders; land laborers; small merchants; and artisans. Some were former oners of landed estates, teachers, civic leaders, university professors, students, and priosts. Disproportionately few were army officers.

The overwhelming majority of all these people were forced to work. Many were employed in forestry and agriculture; a much smaller percentage worked atmining, railroad or highway construction; I and a still smaller group was employed in industry.

The words "fragments of information" are used intentionally. The situation in which the deportees and forced laborers found themselves made any systematic observation of their surroundings most difficult, even for those among then who might have had an understanding of the advisability of looking around and seeing a little more than their guards wanted them to. They always traveled in closed freight and prison cars, were confined to a camp or working area, and had to concentrate on how to survive under the most difficult living conditions. They had no maps and could not make any notes. The only period of freedom of movement was the time of their journey to jein the Polish Army in the fall and winter of 1941-42. They could hardly get the. proper spelling of the names of places where they were confined. In some of the reports and statements, even quite well know geographic names are distorted or misspelled, and geographic directions md names of oblasti are often wrongly indicated (for example, Northeastern European Russia is, in a few statements, described as Siberia). Allorrance has to be made for this involuntary inexactitude, which in almost every case may be corrected by comparing a given text with others describing the same area, camp, or enterprise.
